# 0028 — Factor models with caller-supplied factor returns

**Status:** Accepted — methodology 0.1.0

## Problem

Multi-factor regressions (CAPM with factor data, Fama-French three- and
five-factor, Carhart four-factor, custom factors) separate a fund's return into
compensated exposures and an unexplained intercept (alpha). Several choices
change the numbers: whether the dependent variable is an excess return, how
standard errors treat serial correlation (common in hedge-fund and illiquid
returns), how the Newey-West lag is chosen, which reference distribution is used,
how factor files in percent are scaled, and what happens when factor data have
gaps. Each must be fixed in advance, not chosen per fund.

## Known definitions

For $m$ aligned periods and $k$ factors (notation: [notation](../../reference/notation.md)):

```math
y_t = r_t - r^{\mathrm{RF}}_t = \alpha + \sum_{j=1}^{k} \beta_j f_{j,t} + \varepsilon_t
```

- Fama & French (1993) and Carhart (1997) regress **excess** fund returns on
  excess-return (market) and zero-investment (size, value, momentum) factors.
- Newey & West (1987): heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC)
  covariance with Bartlett weights $1 - \ell / (q + 1)$.
- Newey & West (1994) and statsmodels' default use the lag rule
  $q = \lfloor 4 (m/100)^{2/9} \rfloor$; R `sandwich::NeweyWest` defaults to a
  data-dependent (automatic) bandwidth with prewhitening.

## PerformanceAnalytics behavior

PerformanceAnalytics offers single-index CAPM functions (decision 0012) but no
multi-factor regression with HAC inference. The independent oracle is R
`lm` + `sandwich::NeweyWest(lag = q, prewhite = FALSE, adjust = TRUE)` +
`lmtest::coeftest` (Student t with $m - k - 1$ df). **Verified** on the committed
factor fixture (Fama-French-style factors in percent with an RF column and one
missing HML month): coefficients, HAC standard errors, t-statistics, p-values,
95% intervals, $R^2$, adjusted $R^2$ and residual standard error agree to
$10^{-8}$ relative for the three-factor and CAPM specifications
(`tests/cross_library/test_factor_models.py`, reference generated by
`validation/r/factor_reference.R`).

## QuantStats behavior

QuantStats has no factor regression (it reports single-index alpha/beta only).

## Empyrical behavior

empyrical has no multi-factor regression; pyfolio-reloaded's factor attribution
(`perf_attrib`) takes pre-computed exposures, a different problem. Not compared.

## OpenFactsheet decision

- **Data are supplied, never downloaded.** `ofs.analyze(returns, factors=...)`
  accepts a DataFrame, CSV/Excel path or table text with one column per factor.
  Without factors, every factor-model metric is `not_applicable` with the reason.
- **Risk-free column.** A column named `RF` (any case) is the risk-free rate and is
  subtracted from fund returns; it is not a factor. Without it the dependent
  variable is the fund return (risk-free rate 0%, consistent with
  [0005](0005-risk-free-rate.md)), and the report says so.
- **One return scale for all factor columns.** The percent/decimal classifier of
  [0013](0013-return-scale-detection.md) runs once on the pooled non-RF factor
  values; the decision (or a declared `return_scale`) applies to every column,
  including RF, so a small RF column is never read on a different scale.
- **Alignment.** The periods where the fund and every factor column have a value
  (calendar period for monthly/quarterly/yearly data, exact date otherwise);
  missing values are never filled; different regular frequencies are a
  `FactorAlignmentError` (no resampling). Undated data pair by position only with
  equal lengths, with a warning.
- **Estimator.** OLS with an intercept (statsmodels). Newey-West covariance,
  Bartlett kernel, lag $q = \lfloor 4 (m/100)^{2/9} \rfloor$, no prewhitening,
  small-sample factor $m / (m - k - 1)$; Student t with $m - k - 1$ degrees of
  freedom; two-sided 95% intervals.
- **Metrics.** `factor_alpha` (per-period intercept × P, arithmetic, as in
  [0012](0012-benchmark-alignment-and-relative-metrics.md)), `factor_alpha_t_statistic`,
  `factor_r_squared` (adjusted in details) and `factor_residual_volatility`
  (regression standard error × √P). Every loading, with HAC standard error,
  t, p and interval, is in report section 11 and in `factor_analysis` in the JSON.
- **Identification.** At least $k + 2$ aligned observations (`insufficient_data`
  below). A constant factor or perfectly collinear factors make the loadings
  unidentified (`not_applicable`). A constant fund, or factors that reproduce the
  fund exactly (residual sum of squares $\le m\,(16\,\varepsilon\,\max|y|)^2$), is
  reported as an exact fit: loadings as solved, standard errors 0, t-statistics
  undefined.
- **Confidence.** Policy `factor_regression`: low below 3 years of overlap,
  moderate below 5; overlap below 90% of the fund history caps at moderate;
  smoothing risk high → low, moderate → capped at moderate (smoothed returns bias
  loadings toward zero and alpha upward). A maximum variance inflation factor of
  10 or more, or serially correlated residuals (Ljung-Box p < 0.05, from 10
  observations), adds a warning.
- **Model label.** Recognized column sets are labelled (e.g. "Fama-French
  three-factor model"); any other set is a "Custom factor model". The label never
  changes the estimation.

## Rationale

Excess returns are the specification in which the factor literature defines
alpha, and using the RF column the caller supplies is data, not a tunable. HAC
inference is the practitioner norm for fund regressions because serial
correlation is common; a fixed lag rule avoids the data-dependent bandwidth (and
prewhitening) that would make results depend on an optimizer's choices. Student t
with residual degrees of freedom is conservative in short samples and matches the
R oracle. Joint scale detection prevents the one failure that would silently
corrupt every coefficient: reading RF (tiny in percent) as decimals while the
factors are percent.

## Consequences

- Alpha and loadings describe average exposures over the aligned periods; they do
  not establish causality and change when a different factor set is supplied.
  Reports therefore always show the model, the factor names and the dependent
  variable next to the numbers.
- The benchmark section's single-index alpha (i.i.d. standard errors, raw returns,
  decision 0012) and the factor-model alpha can differ even for a market-only
  factor file: different dependent variable (excess of RF) and different
  inference.
- No factor data are bundled or fetched; users must obtain them (for example from
  the Kenneth R. French Data Library) and pass them explicitly.
